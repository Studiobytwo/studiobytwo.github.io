Positioned at the intersection of wall and ceiling, this work reconsiders the expected behavior of overhead light. Three adjustable light sources extend outward from a single fixed point, suggesting multiple possible paths while remaining structurally bound to one origin.

The light sources can be repositioned, allowing light to be directed toward chosen areas of the space. This creates an active relationship between object, environment, and decision-making.

Rather than occupying the center and distributing light evenly, the work shifts toward the edge, introducing imbalance, direction, and choice into the spatial experience.

The exposed structure removes the idea of a hidden mechanism and instead presents the system openly, emphasizing decision, limitation, and agency.

The work reflects on moments where movement is possible but not taken, where potential exists but is held back. The refusal is not absence, but an active position.

Designed in Rhino. Future versions allow each branch to rotate and come in varying lengths, offering flexible and targeted lighting control.

## How it was made

We built The Refusal in the HFBK workshop. The parts were cut to length on the cold saw, the steel corner plate was filed by hand, and the pivot joint was brazed. Then the pivot sleeves were test-fitted with the carbon fibre rods before assembly. The photos above show the steps in order.

Three 16 mm carbon fibre rods, 2 m, 1.25 m and 0.75 m long, each ending in a 100 mm Orion glass globe in snow white.
